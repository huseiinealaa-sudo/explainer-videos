# takeoff_lift — How does an airplane take off? Lift and takeoff speeds   (target 4:30–4:50, max 5:00; measured narration 4:45.7 after the owner's cuts (a) and (b), 7 segments)

Durations are the measured narration audio (ar-SA-HamedNeural, normal speed). All numbers from
`takeoff_data.py` (MASS, G, WEIGHT, WING_AREA, CL_TAKEOFF, RHO_15, RHO_HOT, T_HOT_C, V_15,
V_15_KMH, V_HOT, HOT_INCREASE, HEADWIND, V_GROUND_HEADWIND, WEIGHT_INCREASE, HEAVY_INCREASE,
LIFT_RATIO_DOUBLE_SPEED). The airliner is generic: no type, no airline, no logo, no registration.

**3D (segments 1 and 5):** the scene class is `TakeoffLift(SyncedScene, ThreeDScene)`. The
airliner is one helper in the script, `airliner_3d()`, built from basic shapes and reused by
both 3D segments: fuselage `Cylinder` along x, nose `Cone` (or a scaled `Sphere` half), tail
cone, swept wings and horizontal tail as flat `Polygon`s, fin as a vertical `Polygon`, two
engine `Cylinder`s under the wings; whiteboard look = white / very light grey fills
(`checkerboard_colors=False`), thin INK strokes; low surface resolution. Runway: long light-grey
rectangle with dashed centreline and threshold bars. Camera moves are slow (`move_camera`,
≥ 3 s each, or a slow ambient rotation). Every text in a 3D segment is fixed in frame
(`add_fixed_in_frame_mobjects`); at the end of each 3D segment the screen is cleared and the
camera goes back to the default top-down view (`phi=0, theta=-90°, zoom=1`) so the 2D segments
look like the rest of the library.

Colours: ACCENT_1 blue = lift and airflow; ACCENT_2 orange = thrust / engines / the pilot's
action; ACCENT_3 green = continue / OK / within; ACCENT_4 red = weight, drag, reject, stall,
the myth. GREY_INK = runway, ground, chord line.
